# the inventory

Every flag `ste-check --register byline` raises, what it is, and what replaces it. Read this when a flag
is unclear, or when adding a new banned form.

## throat-clearing openers

The sentence that warms up before the sentence that says something. Cut the opener, and the
sentence under it becomes the first sentence.

`In today's fast-paced` · `It's worth noting that` · `At its core` · `In this section` ·
`Let's dive in` · `When it comes to` · `It is important to understand` · `First and
foremost` · `In order to better` · `Needless to say`

## hedges

A hedge protects the writer from a claim the writer already believes. Delete it and the
claim stands. Keep a hedge only where the uncertainty is real and material, and then say
what would settle it.

`arguably` · `somewhat` · `fairly` · `quite` · `rather` · `perhaps` · `essentially` ·
`basically` · `virtually` · `relatively` · `generally speaking` · `in some sense`

## adverb padding

An -ly adverb usually marks a weak verb. `runs quickly` is `sprints`. `significantly
improved` is the number. Cap: 2 per 100 words. Over that, the prose is leaning on adverbs
instead of verbs and nouns.

Worst offenders: `significantly` · `dramatically` · `seamlessly` · `effortlessly` ·
`robustly` · `carefully` · `simply` · `easily` · `quickly`

## vague declaratives

A word that sounds like a claim and carries none. Replace with the specific thing, or cut.

`game-changer` · `powerful` · `robust` · `seamless` · `leverage` · `delve` · `landscape` ·
`realm` · `tapestry` · `underscore` · `pivotal` · `crucial` · `cutting-edge` ·
`best-in-class` · `world-class` · `revolutionize` · `unlock` · `elevate` · `streamline`

## meta-commentary

Prose about the prose. A reader who is reading it does not need to be told they are reading
it.

`as mentioned above` · `as we will see` · `this document will` · `in this article` · `the
following section` · `it should be noted` · `for the purposes of this`

## Wh- openers

The rhetorical question shape, dressed as a statement. `What makes this fast is the index`
becomes `The index makes this fast`.

`What makes this` · `Why this matters` · `How this works is` · `What's interesting here`

## the binary contrast

`It's not about X. It's about Y.` and its cousin `not just X, but Y`. Both announce a
profundity the sentence does not contain. State Y.

## dashes between clauses

An em dash or en dash joining two clauses. Use a period, a comma, a colon, or a semicolon.
This matches the house rule the rest of the config already runs on.

## metronomic rhythm

Every sentence landing at the same length reads as generated, whatever the words are.
The check flags a standard deviation under 3 words across sentences. The fix is not padding
one sentence, it is cutting another to a fragment and letting one run long.

## adding to this list

A new form goes in only after it is observed in a real draft. Record the draft it came from
in the same commit. An imagined tell is not evidence, per the weakest-claim rule in
CLAUDE.md.
