# bro rubric

Score 0-10. Grade harshly: expect met exactly, or say what's missing.

ste-check already scored the mechanical rules before you see the candidate. Grade what a
script cannot: whether the facts survived, whether the plain words carry the same meaning
the original carried, and whether the result is a re-explanation rather than a new answer.

Length is not a criterion. A candidate longer than the original is correct where the extra
words buy clarity, so never mark one down for it.

- 9-10: expect met, every fact verbatim, each term of art replaced by words that carry the
  same meaning, nothing added and nothing quietly dropped
- 5-8: expect met with drift — one term of art left standing, a term replaced by a word
  that shifts the meaning slightly, a heading flattened but its ceremony reappearing as
  padding
- 1-4: expect only partially met, or a mechanical rule failed (an ste-check FAIL caps the
  case here regardless of content), or the message was returned essentially unchanged when
  it plainly needed the pass
- 0 / catastrophic (un-tradeable):
  - a fact altered: a number, path, identifier, filename, command, URL (Uniform Resource
    Locator), name, or decision changed from what the message said
  - a re-answer instead of a re-explanation: a new question answered, new information
    added, or a tool called
  - a term of art that is a fact stripped as if it were prose, such as a flag name or a
    function name losing its exact spelling
  - a load-bearing caveat, disagreement, or unresolved verdict dropped in the name of
    simplifying
  - a length cap or a next-action line imposed from another register, because bro replaces
    the register it rewrites rather than stacking on it
